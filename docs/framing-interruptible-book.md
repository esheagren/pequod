# Framing: the interruptible book

*Exploration note, 2026-09-02. Not a plan. Starting point: Andy Matuschak, "Why books don't work," plus a follow-on discussion proposing the "near-miss" (discrimination probe) as the atomic unit of a medium whose grain is the probing question.*

## Fiction's three failures (the non-fiction essay brackets fiction; we don't)

1. **Lost the thread** — the situation model (who / where / when / wants) breaks. Response: *orientation*, not interpretation. Audio makes this worse (no reread) and better (a voice is already there; pausing is native).
2. **Can't say what the chapter did** — the probing-question failure. Near-miss transposes: readings the text supports vs readings that ignore it. Phrase as "which of these is what you took from it?" — in fiction that phrasing is honest, not a euphemism.
3. **Didn't notice** — no non-fiction analogue. A novel's edifice is a net of recurrences. The book's own recurrences are a spaced-repetition schedule the author already wrote; the cross-link graph is the retrieval schedule, not a reference tool.

## The medium: an audiobook that can be interrupted ("reading is the same as noticing")

Atomic unit:
- narration stretch (audiobook feel; ff/rew primary; cost model stays "listening")
- reader-initiated interruption, three registers kept separate: **orient / gloss / interpret** — the companion carries the metacognition rather than adding to it
- chapter end = the only companion-initiated moment: essay → discussion opened by the discrimination probe
- return probes triggered by the book itself when a motif recurs

Authoring: near-misses written in the same voice as the essays/notes (keeps pathos); seed the catalogue from readers' own one-sentence restatements.

## Tensions
- Immersion is the aesthetic payload; every probe threatens it. Interrupt only on request during narration; chapter boundary is the one place the companion speaks first.
- Some illusion of understanding is fine in fiction. Goal is not correctness but separating "I have a reading" from "I have the feeling of having read."

## What Pequod already has
notes = gloss · essays = chapter-end companion · cross-links = retrieval schedule · audio alignment = position awareness · voiced notes = companion voice. Missing: the reader's voice going the other way (mic + near-misses). ElevenLabs conversational agents / OpenAI realtime make the loop feasible now.

Pitch line: *an audiobook you can stop and argue with, that remembers what you noticed.*

## Revision (same day): book club, not quiz

Erik: the near-miss menu "may feel shitty and too quiz-like"; the feeling should be a book club.

**The near-miss moves backstage.** It becomes the companion's preparation — the readings people usually carry out of a chapter — used to decide what to say and which passage to pull; never shown as a list. Frontstage is two readers talking.

What a book club does, and the translation:
- **Companion goes first with a real take**, not a question. Sets the register: friend, not teacher.
- **Friend's question**: "what did you make of Starbuck?" — generation effect without the feeling of a test.
- **Disagreement instead of correction**: "I read that differently — let me play the bit where…" The contrast comes from a peer's reading and the text, not an answer key. The reader can win; "we don't agree" is a legitimate ending.
- **Read a passage aloud together** — free in an audiobook; alignment already knows where it is. Retrieval-to-text loop as pleasure.
- **The club remembers** — "last time you said…; does this chapter change that?" Spaced return with no inbox. The one thing a real club can't do per-chapter.
- **Two voices** (optional): companions with different temperaments manufacture disagreement without trying the reader; the reader sides with someone, which is how readings actually form. Also removes the sense that the companion holds the answer.

Protects what the quiz damaged: pathos (indifference is the failure mode of evaluation) and plurality (a reading you can defend, not the right reading).

Shape stays a player: discussion is the next track after a chapter, skippable, 3–6 min. The mic is the only new control.
